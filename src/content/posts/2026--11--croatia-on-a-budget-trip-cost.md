**Croatia costs roughly $65–110 a day on a tight budget and $160–255 a day at mid-range, flights not included.** The country is cheapest inland and in the shoulder months, and most expensive on the islands and in Dubrovnik in July and August. The trick is to sleep in Zagreb and Split, ride the buses, eat bakery and konoba food, and book park tickets outside summer.

Street food is the budget trick: a bakery *burek* costs about €2–3.50 ($2–4), a pizza slice €2.50–4 and *ćevapi* €8–12 in Split. A main course in a non-tourist konoba (a family-run tavern) runs about €15–20 ($17–22), and a half-litre beer is €3–5. A city bus ticket in Split costs about €1.30–1.50, and a Zagreb tram or bus ride is about €0.53 if you buy ahead. These ranges come from several current travel-cost guides, which disagree by a few dollars, so treat them as planning numbers, not quotes.

Dorm beds vary most by city. One recent guide lists Zagreb dorms at about €20–35 and Split at about €10–50 depending on the month, while peak-summer Hvar runs €40–70 and Dubrovnik is the most expensive of all.

In July and August, plan on the high end or more. A tight budget here means dorm beds and bakery or konoba food. Add 10–15% for extras.

### The route, day by day

#### Days 1–2: Zagreb and Plitvice Lakes

Start in Zagreb, the cheapest big city in the country: dorm beds from about €20 and a day ticket for trams and buses of just under €4. Spend a day walking the Upper Town (free), then take a bus to Plitvice Lakes National Park, a chain of waterfalls and turquoise lakes. Adult tickets cost €10 from November to March, €23.50 in April, May and October, and €40 from June to September, so this is the sight where the season matters most. Book ahead in summer because entry is timed.

#### Days 3–5: Split and Krka

Split is the budget base of the coast. Walk Diocletian's Palace, the Roman-era old town, for free, then pay for the extras you want: the palace cellars are about €7 and the cathedral bell tower about €6. Use a day for Krka National Park, which costs €7 from November to March, €20 in April, May and October, and €40 from June to September. A guided Krka day tour from Split is about €50–70 plus the park ticket, so going by bus on your own is cheaper. A foot-passenger ferry from Split to Hvar Town costs €8.50 on the fast Krilo boat, but Hvar beds are expensive in summer, so make it a day trip.

#### Days 6–7: Dubrovnik

The bus from Split to Dubrovnik takes about 3 hours 45 minutes, and FlixBus fares start from about €6 if you book early. The old town's city walls are the big expense: €40 from March to October and €15 from November to February, and the ticket also covers Fort Lovrijenac. Walk the walls in the morning, then eat outside the old town, where prices are lower than inside the walls (a beer can cost up to €8 there).

## Cheapest time to go

The cheapest months are November to March, when park and wall tickets drop by about two thirds, but many coastal restaurants, ferries and attractions run reduced hours or close, and Dubrovnik's walls close at about 15:00. April, May and October are the best balance: tickets are mid-priced and most things are open. July and August are the most expensive, with the highest beds, ferries and park prices.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in Croatia?** About $425–690 for a tight budget with dorm beds and $980–1,550 at mid-range, excluding flights, based on the route above. The low end needs a quieter month.

**Is Croatia expensive?** It can be, but mostly in July and August, on the islands and in Dubrovnik. Zagreb, Split outside summer, bakery food and intercity buses keep a day cheap.

**Is Croatia cheaper in the off-season?** Yes. Plitvice drops from €40 to €10 and the Dubrovnik walls from €40 to €15 in winter, and beds are cheaper, but some services close, so check opening hours.

**Do I need to book Plitvice in advance?** Booking ahead is recommended in summer because entry is timed, and an online ticket saves queuing.

**Can I get from Split to Dubrovnik cheaply?** Yes. FlixBus runs at least seven trips a day, takes about 3 hours 45 minutes and starts from about €6 when booked early.
