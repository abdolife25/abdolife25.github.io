## Is Italy expensive?

It depends almost entirely on where you sleep and when you go. A hostel bed is about $28–38 and a sit-down pasta lunch about $16, while a mid-range central hotel can pass $130 a night in busy months. Food is genuinely good value: a coffee at the counter is under $2.50, and a two-person meal with a starter, two mains and house wine runs about $33–44.

These ranges come from several travel-cost guides, which differ by a few dollars, so treat them as planning numbers, not quotes.

## What 7 days costs: Rome, Florence and Venice

The classic first trip: three nights in Rome, two in Florence and one in Venice, flying into Rome and out of Venice so you never backtrack.

Venice is the most expensive stop for beds, which is why it gets one night here.

### The route, day by day

#### Days 1–3: Rome

Colosseum, Roman Forum and Palatine Hill on one shared ticket (€18, about $20, valid 24 hours), the Pantheon (it now charges entry, so check the current price), the Vatican Museums (€20, about $22, plus €8 for an audio guide) and a long evening walk through Trastevere. Since 1 February 2026 non-residents are reported to pay €2 to step up to the Trevi Fountain basin, so enjoy it from the square for free.

#### Days 4–5: Florence

Rome to Florence is a short high-speed train ride (about 1.5 hours). Walk the Duomo area and Ponte Vecchio (free), see Michelangelo's David at the Accademia (€16, about $18) or the Uffizi (€29 online, about $32), and climb to Piazzale Michelangelo for sunset (free).

#### Days 6–7: Venice

Florence to Venice is another high-speed ride of about 2 hours. Wander the car-free lanes, take the public *vaporetto* instead of a gondola, and skip the Doge's Palace (€35, about $38) if you are on a tight budget. Day visitors may have to pay a €5 entry fee on peak days; it applied on 60 days from April to July in 2026, and the 2027 dates are not announced yet, so check the official Venice portal before you go. Overnight guests are exempt but may still need to register their booking.

## Trains in Italy: what they really cost

Book high-speed trains early. Trenitalia's cheapest advance fares are about €19.90 for Rome–Florence and Florence–Venice (about $22 each), but they are non-refundable and limited to one train; the flexible fare is roughly €50–57 (about $55–63). Rome to Venice directly runs from about €29.90 (about $33) up to €92 or more on the day. Slower regional trains have a fixed low price and do not need advance booking, so if you are in no hurry they are the cheapest way to hop between cities. Check Trenitalia and Italo side by side before you buy; both run Rome–Florence–Venice.

## Cheapest time to go

Low season is roughly November to February, when flights and hotels are cheapest, but it is colder and wetter, and some tours run reduced hours. Christmas, New Year and Carnival in Venice are the exceptions, with higher prices. Shoulder months, such as April to May and September to October, still offer deals with better weather. Summer and Easter are the busiest, most expensive periods.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in Italy?** About $535–815 for a tight budget and $1,085–1,620 at mid-range, excluding flights, based on the route above.

**Where does the money go in Italy?** Food and museum tickets are good value; central hotels in busy months and last-minute train fares are where costs climb, so book those early.

**Do I need to pay extra to enter Venice?** Overnight guests do not pay the day-visitor fee, but on its peak days you may still have to register. Check the official portal for the 2027 dates before booking.

**Can I do Italy for under $75 a day?** Yes, if you stay in dorms, buy lunch at bakeries and counters, walk between sights and use regional trains. Add high-speed trains and mid-priced sights and it moves toward $100.
