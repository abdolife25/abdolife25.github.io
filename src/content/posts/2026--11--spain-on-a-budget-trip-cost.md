**Spain costs roughly $60–100 a day on a tight budget and $140–230 a day at mid-range, flights not included.** It is cheapest in Seville and Granada and outside July and August, and most expensive in Barcelona and on the islands in summer. The budget tricks are the same everywhere: sleep in a dorm, eat the *menú del día* (set lunch), book high-speed trains early and pay for only the two or three sights you really want.

## What Spain costs per day (2027)

| | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Bed | Hostel dorm $20–38 | Share a 3-star double $40–140 each |
| Food | $22–35 | $50–75 |
| Local transport | $4–7 | $13–20 |
| Sights | $5–15 | $15–30 |
| **Per day** | **about $60–100** | **about $140–230** |

The *menú del día* is the budget trick: three courses, a drink and bread for about €12–18 ($13–20) on weekdays. Paid tapas run about €2–8 ($2.20–8.80) each in Madrid, Barcelona and Seville, while in Granada and a few other cities a tapa comes free with a drink of about €2.50 ($2.75). One thing to know: Catalonia (including Barcelona) adds a tourist tax of about €2.25–4 ($2.50–4.40) per person per night, so ask whether it is already in your price.

Dorm prices vary by city. One guide lists Madrid dorms at €20–30, Barcelona €25–35, Seville €18–28 and Granada €15–25, while another puts a dorm bed at about $13–16, so treat these as planning ranges, not quotes. Mid-range hotels are listed at about €120–200 in Madrid, €150–250 in Barcelona and €70–120 in Seville per room per night, which is why Seville and Granada are the cheap bases.

## What 7 days costs in Spain

Seven days and six nights on a Barcelona, Madrid and Seville route using high-speed trains: two nights in each city.

| Cost (6 nights, 7 days) | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Beds and tourist tax | $145–215 | $380–630 |
| Food | $155–245 | $350–525 |
| Trains and local transport | $80–155 | $140–250 |
| Sights | $50–70 | $120–220 |
| **Total** | **about $430–685** | **about $990–1,625** |

The low end assumes dorm beds, trains booked well ahead and the set lunch as your main meal. Mid-range assumes a shared double in a 3-star hotel (the beds line is per person). In July and August expect the high end or more in Barcelona and on the islands. Add 10–15% for extras. As a check, one backpacker guide puts a social, hostel-based week at about $472 and another guide puts the average traveler at about $135 a day, both inside these ranges.

### The route, day by day

#### Days 1–2: Barcelona

Fly into Barcelona. The T-Casual card gives 10 rides on metro and buses for about €12.55 ($13.80), which covers two days easily. The big sight is the Sagrada Família: €26 ($29) for the basilica with the audioguide app, or €36 ($40) with the tower, which you climb by lift and leave by a narrow spiral staircase of about 300 steps. It sells out two to four weeks ahead in peak season, so book on the official site. Park Güell's paid Monumental Zone costs €18 ($20) for timed entry, but the wooded parts and the Turó de les Tres Creus viewpoint are free with no booking. Casa Batlló starts from about €29 ($32) online and La Pedrera from €25 ($28), so most budget travelers pick one or skip both and walk the Gothic Quarter and the beach for free.

#### Days 3–4: Madrid

Take the high-speed train to Madrid (about 2.5 to 3.5 hours). Booked ahead, one traveler paid just €19 ($21) on Ouigo, while AVE and same-day fares cost more, so compare Renfe, Ouigo, Iryo and Avlo. In Madrid a 10-trip metro card is about €12.20 ($13.40). The Prado costs €15 ($16.50) and is free in the last two hours daily (Monday to Saturday 6–8 pm, Sunday 5–7 pm), and the Reina Sofía is free after 7 pm on Monday and Wednesday to Saturday. Plan your evenings around those free slots, and eat a set lunch near the museums.

#### Days 5–6: Seville

Take the Madrid–Seville high-speed train (about 2 hours 30 minutes). Booked in advance it starts from about €28 ($31), while buying on the day can cost about €75 ($83), the biggest saving on this route. Seville is the cheapest of the three cities, with dorms around €18–28 ($20–31) and mid-range hotels about €70–120 ($77–132) a night. Spend the days walking the old quarter and eating tapas, and check the Alcázar's opening and free-entry hours on its official page before you go, because they change.

#### Day 7: home from Seville

Fly out of Seville, or take the train back to Madrid if your flight is from there. Booking an open-jaw flight (into Barcelona, out of Seville) saves a day of backtracking, so check that when you search.

## Cheapest time to go

Outside July and August prices drop, and winter is the cheapest season for beds, though some northern and island resorts are quiet. Spring and autumn are the best balance of weather and price. One guide notes that the cheapest month for flights from the US is September and the most expensive is late June, so check your own route. Book Sagrada Família and any other timed ticket early in spring, summer and around Easter and Christmas.

## Planning Spain from your country

For most visitors the flight is the biggest single cost, so check fares from your own airport and compare more than one airline. Entry rules depend on your passport, so check the official Spanish and EU border pages before you book. Train fares move with demand, so confirm them on Renfe, Ouigo and Iryo before you go. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in Spain?** About $430–685 for a tight budget with dorm beds and $990–1,625 at mid-range, excluding flights, based on the Barcelona, Madrid and Seville route above.

**Is Spain cheap?** It is one of Western Europe's cheaper countries, especially Seville, Granada, the set lunch and the trains if you book ahead. Barcelona and the islands in July and August are where costs climb.

**How much is the train from Madrid to Seville?** Booked in advance it starts from about €28 ($31), and buying on the day can cost about €75 ($83). It takes about 2 hours 30 minutes.

**Do I need to book the Sagrada Família in advance?** Yes. The official site opens slots about 60 days ahead and peak-season dates sell out two to four weeks before, so book as soon as you know your dates.

**What is free in Madrid and Barcelona?** The Prado is free in its last two hours daily, the Reina Sofía is free on most evenings after 7 pm, and Park Güell's wooded areas are free with no booking.
