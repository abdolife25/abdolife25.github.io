**The Canary Islands cost roughly $55–80 a day on a tight budget and $120–175 a day at mid-range, flights not included, based on Tenerife, the biggest and most visited island.** Tenerife is cheapest in May, June, September and October and most expensive around Carnaval in February. The savings come from dorm beds, a bus pass instead of a rental car, local *guachinche* meals and the free sights: beaches, natural ocean pools and the Teide hiking trails.

## What Tenerife costs per day (2027)

| | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Bed | Hostel dorm $24–31 | Share a 3-star double $47–62 each |
| Food | $25–35 | $45–60 |
| Local transport | $9–12 | $24–35 |
| Sights | $0–7 | $15–30 |
| **Per day** | **about $55–80** | **about $120–175** |

Food is where the islands are kind to your budget. A coffee is about €1.50–2 ($1.65–2.20), a *ración* of cheese with *mojo* sauce from about €5 ($5.50), a cheap sit-down meal about €15 ($17), and the local *guachinches* (rustic home-style restaurants in the north) serve meals for about €8–15 ($9–17). Farmers' markets such as the Mercado de Nuestra Señora de África in Santa Cruz are good for cheap picnic supplies.

Beds are priced by area. Dorm beds are about €22–28 ($24–31) in Santa Cruz and La Laguna and from about €20 ($22) in a surf hostel, and a hotel room for two starts from about €90 ($99) a night. A mid-range hotel room averages about $94–123 a night in one guide, so sharing halves it. Resort areas in the south cost more than the north and the capital.

## What 7 days costs in Tenerife

Seven days and six nights based in Santa Cruz or La Laguna in the north, with day trips by bus, plus a Teide day and a southern beach day. Mid-range adds three days of car hire.

| Cost (6 nights, 7 days) | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Beds | $145–185 | $280–370 |
| Food | $175–245 | $315–420 |
| Buses (or hire car) and local transport | $65–85 | $165–240 |
| Sights | $0–50 | $100–200 |
| **Total** | **about $385–565** | **about $860–1,230** |

The low end assumes dorm beds, a seven-day bus card, the free Teide hike and guachinche meals, in spring or autumn. Mid-range assumes a shared double (the beds line is per person) and three days of car hire at about €30 ($33) a day, plus fuel. Prices rise around Carnaval in February, when beds in Santa Cruz and Las Palmas jump. As a check, one guide puts the average traveler at about $139 (€128) a day across all styles, which sits inside our mid-range.

### The week, day by day

#### Days 1–2: Santa Cruz and La Laguna

From Tenerife South Airport a TITSA bus to Santa Cruz costs about €9.35 ($10.30) and to Los Cristianos about €3.20 ($3.50). A single bus ticket is from about €1.25 ($1.40), a day travelcard is about €10 ($11) and a seven-day card about €50 ($55), each plus a card fee of about €2 ($2.20). The capital's market bars and the historic centre of La Laguna are cheap and free to walk, and the Museo de Naturaleza y Arqueología in Santa Cruz is free from 4 pm on Fridays and Saturdays.

#### Day 3: Mount Teide

Teide is the island's big day out. The cable car costs €43 ($47) return for a non-resident adult (€24 or $26 one way), and it does not include the permit for the final climb to the crater at 3,718 m, which is free through the National Park's website and has to be booked well ahead. Many budget travelers hike the free trails instead and skip the cable car, or take it one way. Check the cable car's seasonal opening hours before you go.

#### Days 4–5: the north coast and south beaches

Spend a day on the north coast, where the natural ocean pools are free and guachinches serve cheap local lunches, and a day in the south around Los Cristianos, which the airport bus reaches cheaply. If you want a big paid day, Siam Park is €44 ($48) for adults and €32 ($35) for children aged 3–11, and Loro Parque is €42 ($46) for adults and €30 ($33) for children up to eleven. Both are optional, and most budget travelers pick one or neither.

#### Days 6–7: free hikes and home

Use the last days for free hikes and coastal walks, or take a ferry to La Gomera if you want a second island. Fly home from either airport.

## Cheapest time to go

May, June and September are the cheapest months, with prices about 20–30% below peak, and May and October are listed as the best value, with May weather around 24°C (75°F) and hotel rates about €10–15 cheaper. Spring and autumn also bring cheaper car hire. Avoid Carnaval in February, when beds in Santa Cruz and Las Palmas rise sharply.

## Planning the Canary Islands from your country

For most visitors the flight is the biggest single cost, so check fares from your own airport and compare more than one airline. The Canary Islands are part of Spain, so entry rules follow Spain's, which depend on your passport; check the official Spanish and EU border pages before you book. Bus prices and cable car prices change, so confirm them on the TITSA and Teide cable car websites. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in the Canary Islands?** About $385–565 for a tight budget with dorm beds and $860–1,230 at mid-range, excluding flights, based on a week in Tenerife.

**Is Tenerife cheap?** It is cheaper than most of Western Europe for food and local transport, and the free beaches, ocean pools and hikes keep sights cheap. The southern resorts, Carnaval and the winter peak are where costs climb.

**How much is the Teide cable car?** €43 ($47) return for a non-resident adult, or €24 ($26) one way. It does not include the free permit you need to climb to the crater, so book that early.

**Do I need a car in Tenerife?** Not for the main towns, because TITSA buses cover the island and a seven-day card is about €50 ($55). A car at about €30 ($33) a day can be cheaper for two or three people sharing.

**Which Canary Island is cheapest?** This guide covers Tenerife only. Gran Canaria, Lanzarote and the other islands have their own prices, so check them before you book.
