**Egypt costs roughly $40–85 a day on a tight budget and $95–180 a day at mid-range, flights and long-distance trains not included.** Food, beds and local transport are cheap. The big costs are foreigner ticket prices at the major sights and the overnight train to Luxor. The trick is to base yourself in a budget hotel, eat where locals eat, and choose which paid sights are worth it, because the Grand Egyptian Museum alone is now about $34.

Sight tickets are set in Egyptian pounds.*

## What Egypt costs per day (2027)

| | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Bed | Hostel bed or budget room $15–35 | Share a 3–4 star double $25–60 each |
| Food | $10–20 | $35–60 |
| Local transport | $3–8 | $8–18 |
| Sights (weekly average) | $13–20 | $26–41 |
| **Per day** | **about $40–85** | **about $95–180** |

These daily figures leave out the overnight train between Cairo and Luxor, which is priced separately below. Local food is where Egypt is cheapest: a *koshary* meal (rice, lentils and pasta) is about $1.70, street food and local restaurants run about $2–8 a serving, and a sit-down tourist restaurant main is about $15–35. A beer is about $3.80 and only in licensed venues. Many sit-down restaurants add about 26% service charge and VAT to the menu price, so check the bill.

Getting around the cities is cheap too. A Cairo metro ticket is about $0.16–0.42, an Uber from downtown to Giza is about $5, and an airport transfer is about $12–15. In Luxor, the Nile ferry is EGP 10 (under $0.20), and a short taxi ride is about EGP 80–150 ($1.50–3), so agree the fare first.

Small tips (*baksheesh*) are expected for small services, about EGP 40–50 (about $1) each. Many nationalities also need a visa, and an e-visa is about $25 booked in advance, so check your passport before you go.

## What 7 days costs in Egypt

Seven days and six nights: two nights in Cairo, the sleeper train to Luxor, two nights in Luxor, and the sleeper train back to Cairo on your last night. This route uses the sleepers as two of your six beds.

| Cost (6 nights, 7 days) | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Beds (4 hotel nights) | $60–140 | $100–240 |
| Food | $70–140 | $245–420 |
| Trains, transfers and local transport | $270–305 | $340–460 |
| Sights | $89–137 | $185–285 |
| **Total** | **about $490–720** | **about $870–1,400** |

The train line is the biggest single cost: a shared two-berth Standard sleeper is about $110 each way and an Elite sleeper about $125, and foreigners pay more than Egyptians. The low end uses Standard and cheap local transport, and the mid-range uses Elite plus private taxis. The sights line covers the six core tickets in the low case (Giza Plateau, the Grand Egyptian Museum, Karnak, Luxor Temple, the Valley of the Kings and Hatshepsut's temple) and adds the Citadel, the Egyptian Museum and the Great Pyramid interior in the high case; mid-range adds private guides. Add 10–15% for extras.

### The route, day by day

#### Days 1–2: Cairo and Giza

Land in Cairo and spend the first day in Islamic Cairo: Khan el-Khalili bazaar is free to walk, and the Citadel is about $10. On day 2 take an Uber to the Giza Plateau (about $5 from downtown) and buy the $13 plateau ticket, with the Great Pyramid interior as an optional $28 extra. Go early in the day to avoid the heat and the crowds.

#### Day 3: Grand Egyptian Museum, then the night train

Spend the morning at the Grand Egyptian Museum, which is the priciest single ticket on this trip at about $34, and add the older Egyptian Museum in Tahrir for about $10 if you have time. In the evening, take the sleeper from Cairo to Luxor. Sleepers leave from a different station (Cairo Upper Egypt) than the day trains, so allow a taxi or Uber, and arrive 30 minutes early. Sleeper bookings open four to five months ahead, so book early for November to February.

#### Days 4–5: Luxor

Arrive in Luxor in the morning. Spend the east bank day at Karnak (about $11) and Luxor Temple (about $9), and the west bank day at the Valley of the Kings (about $14 for three tombs) and Hatshepsut's temple (about $8). Tutankhamun's tomb is an extra EGP 700 (about $13). A guesthouse on the west bank runs about $18 a night, and a Nile-view dinner is about $18.

#### Days 6–7: Nile day, then back to Cairo

Use day 6 for a free felucca ride or a Nile ferry crossing and a slow walk, then take the sleeper back to Cairo that night and arrive on the morning of day 7 for your flight home. Foreigners cannot buy day-train tickets on the Egyptian Railways site at local prices, so for a day train buy the foreigner fare at the station's foreigner reservation office or through an agency.

## Cheapest time to go

Guides put November, March and October as the best value, with pleasant weather and mid-level prices. December to February is peak season, with prices 30–50% higher. June to August is the cheapest, about 25–40% lower, but temperatures often pass 40°C (104°F), which makes the big outdoor sites hard work.

## Planning Egypt from your country

For most visitors the flight is the biggest single cost, so check fares from your own airport and compare more than one airline. Entry and visa rules depend on your passport, so check the official Egyptian visa pages and your own government's current travel advice before you book. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in Egypt?** About $490–720 for a tight budget and $870–1,400 at mid-range, excluding flights and visa, based on the Cairo and Luxor route above. The sleeper train is the biggest single cost.

**Is Egypt cheap to visit?** Daily costs are low, especially food, local transport and beds. The sight tickets are high for foreigners, and the Grand Egyptian Museum is about $34 alone.

**Why are tickets so expensive for foreigners?** Egypt charges foreigners much higher fares and entry fees than Egyptians for trains and sights. Always buy the foreigner price, because buying a local fare can lead to fines.

**How much is the train from Cairo to Luxor?** The sleeper is about $110 each way for a shared Standard berth and about $125 for Elite. Book through Abela's own site or an agency such as Trainsegypt.

**When is the cheapest time to visit Egypt?** June to August is cheapest but very hot. November, March and October balance weather and price.
