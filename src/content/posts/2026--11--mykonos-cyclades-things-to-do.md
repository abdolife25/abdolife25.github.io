**The best things to do in Mykonos cost very little: wandering Mykonos Town and Little Venice, the windmills at sunset and the beaches are free, and the one big paid day is Delos, which is about $22 for the site plus about $24–28 for the boat.** The money traps are beach sunbeds, a drink with a view and everything in July and August. This guide ranks what is worth paying for, with prices in US dollars.

## The best things to do in Mykonos, ranked by value

| Rank | What | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mykonos Town, Little Venice and the Paraportiani church | Free | Half a day |
| 2 | Kato Mili windmills at sunset | Free | 1 hour |
| 3 | Delos archaeological site | €20 ($22) plus boat | Half a day |
| 4 | A beach day on the free part of the sand | Free (sunbeds extra) | A day |
| 5 | Archaeological Museum of Mykonos | €5 ($5.50) | 1 hour |
| 6 | Ano Mera village and Panagia Tourliani monastery | Free (donation) | 1–2 hours |

**1. Mykonos Town and Little Venice.** The maze of white streets in Chora costs nothing, and neither does walking the Little Venice waterfront or looking at the Panagia Paraportiani church from outside, which is best in late afternoon light. Sitting down at a bar in Little Venice costs money, so walk it, then have a drink elsewhere.

**2. The windmills at Kato Mili.** Watching the sun go down from the hill is free and is the island's signature view. You cannot go inside.

**3. Delos.** The ancient island is the one big paid sight. The site and its museum cost €20 ($22) full price and €10 ($11) reduced on the official Cyclades Ephorate page. Boats leave Mykonos daily for about €22–25 ($24–28) round trip, and a half-day guided tour with the boat and skip-the-line entry is about €65 ($72). The site closes in winter (December to March), and in November the hours are 08:00 to 16:00. Entry is free for all visitors on the last weekend of September and on 18 April, 18 May and 28 October, so those dates are worth planning around, though boats and crowds still apply.

**4. The beaches.** Greek beaches are public up to the waterline, so you can swim for free, even past rows of loungers. Sunbeds are where the money goes: a beach chair and umbrella on a typical Greek beach is about €25 ($28) for two chairs and one umbrella, and some Mykonos beaches charge around €80 ($88) for sunbeds. If you bring a towel and sit on the free sand, a beach day costs nothing.

**5. The Archaeological Museum of Mykonos.** €5 ($5.50) full price, closed on Tuesdays and open about 09:00 to 16:00 in summer. It is free on the first and third Sunday of each month from November to March.

**6. Ano Mera.** The village square and the courtyard of the Panagia Tourliani monastery are free, with a small donation suggested. It is a quieter side of the island.

## What to skip or pay for carefully

- **Cocktails and beach clubs.** A cocktail is €12–20 ($13–22) and a beer €6–7 ($6.60–7.70). Pay for one sunset drink, not a day of them.
- **Taxis.** A ride from the airport to Mykonos Town is about €25 ($28), and taxis are described as expensive. The local bus is about €1.60–2.50 ($1.75–2.75) a ride, and the SeaBus between the new and old port about €2.50 ($2.75).
- **ATV hire.** About €30–60 ($33–66) a day. The bus does the same job for much less.
- **Peak summer.** Prices from June to August can triple against April, May, September and October.

## A one-day route for about $65

Morning: ferry from Mykonos Town to Delos (about $26) and the site ticket ($22). Lunch: street food at about €5–7 ($5.50–7.70). Afternoon: wander Chora and Little Venice. Sunset: the windmills at Kato Mili, which are free. Dinner: street food again, or a taverna meal of about €17–25 ($19–28). Add about $3 for a bus or the SeaBus. That comes to about $65 with street food and about $75–90 with a sit-down dinner.

## Getting there and beyond Mykonos

Most visitors arrive by ferry from Athens. The cheapest Piraeus to Mykonos sailings start at about €43 ($47), with the highest fares near €90 ($99); slow ferries take about 4.5 hours and high-speed ones about 2.5 hours. Other Cyclades islands are reached the same way: a longer Athens to Milos crossing is about €70 ($77), and general island ferry fares run about €25–70 ($28–77). Overnight boats can save up to half. Ferry prices and timetables change by month, so check Ferryhopper for your date.

## Cheapest time to go

April, May, September and October are the cheapest and best months, with lower prices and fewer crowds. Winter is mild and cheap, but many businesses close, Delos is shut from December to March and rain is frequent.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much is Delos?** €20 ($22) for the site and museum at full price, plus about €22–25 ($24–28) for the round-trip boat from Mykonos.

**Is Mykonos expensive?** It can be, especially from June to August, when prices can triple. The free sights, buses and a taverna lunch keep it affordable, and spring and autumn are far cheaper.

**Are Mykonos beaches free?** Yes, the beaches are public up to the waterline. Sunbeds and umbrellas cost extra, from about €25 ($28) to around €80 ($88) at some beaches.

**Do I need a car on Mykonos?** No. Buses cost about €1.60–2.50 ($1.75–2.75) a ride and a SeaBus links the two ports. A car or ATV is optional.

**Is tap water safe to drink?** One guide says it is not, so buy bottled water at about €0.50 ($0.55) in a shop.
