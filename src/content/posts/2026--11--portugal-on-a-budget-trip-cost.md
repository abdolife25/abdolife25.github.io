**Portugal costs roughly $50–100 a day on a tight budget and $115–220 a day at mid-range, flights not included.** It is cheapest in Porto and the off-season, and most expensive in Lisbon and the Algarve in July and August. The trick is to sleep in dorms, eat the daily lunch special, book trains ahead and pick two or three paid sights instead of ten.

## What Portugal costs per day (2027)

| | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Bed | Hostel dorm $22–44 | Share a 3-star double $50–94 each |
| Food | $17–28 | $39–66 |
| Local transport | $4–11 | $9–22 |
| Sights | $6–17 | $17–39 |
| **Per day** | **about $50–100** | **about $115–220** |

The *prato do dia* (a set lunch of a main, drink and often coffee) is the budget trick: about €9–16 ($10–18), and Porto guides put it around €11. A *bifana* (pork sandwich) is about €3.50–4.50 ($4–5), a *pastel de nata* custard tart is about €1.30 ($1.45), an espresso is about €1 ($1.10) and a beer is €2–4 ($2.20–4.40). A mid-range dinner with wine is about €25–35 ($28–38) per person. One thing to know: if the waiter brings bread and olives (*couvert*) and you eat them, you are charged, about €2.50 ($2.75) per person.

Dorm beds vary by city. One recent guide lists Lisbon dorms at €30–45, while Porto has beds from about €14 and Lagos about €15–25, so Porto and the Algarve in the low season are the cheapest bases. Another Lisbon guide puts dorms at €20–35, so treat these as planning ranges, not quotes.

In most towns a night in a bed also carries a city tax: Lisbon charges €4 per person per night and Porto €3, both for the first 7 nights, and the Algarve towns charge about €1 in low season and €2 in high season (it varies by municipality). Ask whether it is already in the price.

## What 7 days costs in Portugal

Seven days and six nights on a Porto, Lisbon and Algarve route (Lagos), using trains and entering the main sights. Two nights in Porto, two in Lisbon (with a day trip to Sintra) and two in Lagos.

| Cost (6 nights, 7 days) | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Beds and city tax | $150–285 | $315–580 |
| Food | $115–195 | $270–460 |
| Trains and local transport | $85–120 | $140–280 |
| Sights and day trips | $40–95 | $115–270 |
| **Total** | **about $390–695** | **about $840–1,590** |

The low end assumes dorm beds, booked-ahead trains and travel between November and March, or at least outside July and August. In July and August, plan on the high end or more: guides put Lisbon and Porto 30–50% higher in peak season and the Algarve 40–60% higher. A tight budget here means dorms, lunch specials and two or three paid sights. Add 10–15% for extras.

### The route, day by day

#### Days 1–2: Porto

Fly into Porto, the cheapest of the big three. Dorm beds start from about €14, a single metro ticket is about €1.40 and a 24-hour Andante transport pass is about €7.50. The airport metro line costs about €2.30. Walk the Ribeira riverfront and cross the Dom Luís I bridge for free, then pay for what you want: Livraria Lello bookshop is about €10 (deducted if you buy a book), the Clérigos Tower about €10 and a classic Sandeman port cellar tour about €23. A full-day Douro Valley trip is about €85 in total if you go with a tour.

#### Days 3–4: Lisbon and Sintra

Take the train from Porto to Lisbon (about 3 hours on the Alfa Pendular). Booked ahead, fares start around €21 for early departures and €34 for mid-morning ones, while the full fare is about €56–71, so book early. In Lisbon, a single metro ride is about €1.90 and a 24-hour Carris and Metro pass is about €7.25. Belém is the big-ticket area: Jerónimos Monastery is €18 and Belém Tower €15 since January 2025, so most budget travelers pick one. Spend the next day in Sintra: the Pena Palace costs €20 with the park or €12 for the park only, and the palace entry is timed, with no late entry and no refund if you miss your slot, so book on the official Parques de Sintra site and arrive early.

#### Days 5–6: Lagos and the Algarve

Take an Intercidades train from Lisbon to Faro (about 3 hours 40 minutes), then a regional train to Lagos (about 1 hour 45 minutes). Booked ahead, the Lisbon–Faro leg was about €25–30, with a full fare of about €49, and the Faro–Lagos train is about €7.70. Lagos is a budget Algarve base: dorms around €15–25, a beer about €2 and a local bus ride €1.20–1.60. It is a long day, so plan it as your transfer day. Spend the next day on the beaches and cliff paths, which are free, and compare boat trips to the caves before booking.

#### Day 7: home from Faro

Take the train back to Faro and fly out. Booking an open-jaw flight (into Porto, out of Faro) saves you a day of backtracking, so check that on your search.

## Cheapest time to go

The cheapest months are November to March (excluding the Christmas weeks), when guides put prices 25–50% below peak, though the Algarve is quieter and some beach services close. April, May, September and October are the best balance: temperatures in the Algarve are around 22–25°C (72–77°F) and prices are mid-range. July and August are the most expensive, especially in the Algarve, and prices also climb around festivals.

## Planning Portugal from your country

For most visitors the flight is the biggest single cost, so check fares from your own airport and compare more than one airline. Entry rules depend on your passport, so check the official Portuguese and EU border pages before you book. Train fares change often, so confirm on the CP website before you go. Use Multibanco ATMs instead of the stand-alone Euronet machines, which charge extra fees. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in Portugal?** About $390–695 for a tight budget with dorm beds and $840–1,590 at mid-range, excluding flights, based on the Porto, Lisbon and Lagos route above. The low end needs a quieter month.

**Is Portugal cheap?** It is one of Western Europe's cheaper countries, especially Porto, the lunch specials and the trains if you book ahead. Lisbon and the Algarve in July and August are where costs climb.

**How much is the train from Lisbon to Porto?** Booked ahead it starts around €21–34 ($23–37), and the full fare is about €56–71 ($62–78). The Alfa Pendular takes about 3 hours.

**Do I need to book the Pena Palace in advance?** Yes. Your palace entry is for a set time slot, and if you arrive late you will not get in or get a refund, so book on the official Parques de Sintra site.

**Which is cheaper, Lisbon or Porto?** Porto. Guides put a Porto dorm bed from about €14 and a daily budget of about €45, against about €65–75 a day in Lisbon.
