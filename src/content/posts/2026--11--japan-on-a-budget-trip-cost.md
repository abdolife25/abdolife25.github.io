## Is Japan expensive?

Not the way its reputation suggests. For many travelers the flight is the biggest single cost. On the ground, a bowl of ramen is about $3–6 (¥500–900), a convenience-store breakfast is under $4, and a dorm bed is $19–38 (¥3,000–6,000). What does add up is intercity trains and hotels in busy seasons, which is where this guide spends most of its time.

These are estimates compiled from several travel-cost guides, which disagree by a few dollars either way, so treat the ranges as planning numbers, not quotes.

## What 7 days costs: Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka

This is the classic first-trip route: three nights in Tokyo, two in Kyoto, two in Osaka, with a day trip to Nara. Fly into Tokyo and out of Osaka (Kansai) so you never backtrack.

| Cost (6 nights, 7 days) | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Beds | $170–225 | $375–530 |
| Food | $130–200 | $265–395 |
| Local transport | $25–45 | $45–63 |
| Tokyo → Kyoto → Osaka trains | about $95 | about $95 |
| Sights and temples | $40–63 | $63–95 |
| **Total** | **about $460–625** | **about $845–1,175** |

The Tokyo–Kyoto bullet train is about $85 for an unreserved seat and $91 reserved (¥13,500 and ¥14,500), and Kyoto to Osaka is a short, cheap rapid-train ride of a few hundred yen. Your biggest savings are the bed and the food: dorms and convenience stores or ramen and conveyor-belt sushi shops hold daily spending down without feeling like sacrifice.

### The route, day by day

#### Days 1–3: Tokyo

Asakusa and Senso-ji (free to walk around), Shibuya Crossing, the Imperial Palace East Garden (free), Akihabara, and the free observation deck of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building. Stay near Asakusa or Ueno for cheap beds and easy trains.

## Do you need the JR Pass?

The Japan Rail Pass (JR Pass) is a prepaid ticket sold to foreign visitors that gives unlimited rides on most JR trains for 7, 14 or 21 days, including most shinkansen (bullet trains) but not the fastest Nozomi and Mizuho services, which need an extra payment. You buy it before you travel (stations inside Japan stopped selling it in 2023), collect it with your passport at a major JR station, then ride through the ticket gates instead of paying for each ticket. Riding Nozomi on Tokyo–Kyoto would add about $31 (¥4,960) on top. It is great for long, fast trips across the country but can cost more than the tickets you would actually use.

**Do you need it?** Probably not for this route. From 1 October 2026 the JR Pass costs about $333 for 7 days in Ordinary class, $528 for 14 days and $660 for 21 days (the 7-day Green Car pass is about $465). Tokyo to Kyoto plus Kyoto to Osaka costs about $95 in tickets, so the pass only pays off if you take several long shinkansen rides in one week, for example Tokyo to Hiroshima and back. Compare your own planned trips before you buy; for a Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka trip, single tickets and an IC card (Suica or ICOCA) cost far less.

## Cheapest time to go

Mid-January to early March is the cheapest stretch, after New Year and before cherry blossom. Late May to June (the rainy season in Tokyo runs roughly 7 June to 19 July) and September to mid-October are good value too, though typhoons are possible in autumn. The most expensive times are cherry blossom season (late March to early April), Golden Week (29 April and 3–5 May in 2027), November leaf season in Kyoto and the New Year holidays.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in Japan?** About $460–625 for a tight budget and $845–1,175 at mid-range, excluding flights, based on the route above.

**Is Japan cheaper now than before?** For visitors paying in dollars, euros or pounds, yes: the yen is weak, so the same yen price costs fewer dollars than a few years ago. Prices inside Japan have risen slightly, so your savings come mostly from the exchange rate.

**Is cash still needed?** Many places accept cards and IC cards, but small restaurants, shrines and some hostels still prefer cash, so carry a few thousand yen.

**Can I do Japan for under $50 a day?** Only if you stay in dorms, eat convenience-store and noodle-shop meals, and avoid long train rides. Add intercity trains and it moves toward $65.
