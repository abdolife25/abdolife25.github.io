**Greece costs roughly $70–115 a day on a tight budget and $135–185 a day at mid-range, flights not included, based on three nights in Athens and three on one Cyclades island.** It is cheapest in Athens and on the mainland and in April, May, September and October, and most expensive on Mykonos and Santorini in July and August. The budget tricks are dorm beds, gyros and set lunches, slow ferries booked ahead and a short list of paid sights.

## What Greece costs per day (2027)

| | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Bed (averaged over the week) | Hostel dorm $21–35 | Share a hotel double $41–52 each |
| Food | $24–39 | $44–60 |
| Local transport and ferries | $18–28 | $26–41 |
| Sights | $7–14 | $22–30 |
| **Per day** | **about $70–115** | **about $135–185** |

Food is kind to a budget. A gyro or souvlaki pita is about €3.50 ($3.85), a lunch special about €10–12 ($11–13), a Greek main about €8–12 ($9–13) and a seafood dinner about €15–20 ($17–22). A sit-down meal with a starter, a main and a glass of wine runs about €20 ($22) in Athens and about €35 ($38) on islands such as Santorini.

Dorms are the big difference between the mainland and the islands. One guide puts a dorm bed at about €20 ($22) in Athens and about €40 ($44) on most islands, while another gives €15–25 on the mainland. Mid-range hotel rooms run about €75–100 ($83–110) in Athens and €100–120 ($110–132) on the islands, which is why the sums above split a double between two people.

One extra to know about: since January 2025 Greece charges a climate resilience fee per night on the bill. In summer (April to October) it is about €2 ($2.20) for a 1–2 star hotel or furnished room, €5 ($5.50) for a 3-star hotel and €8 ($8.80) for a short-term rental, and in winter it drops to €0.50–2. Ask whether it is already in your price.

## What 7 days costs in Greece

Seven days and six nights, three nights in Athens and three on one Cyclades island, with two ferry rides. The island is the cheaper kind, not Mykonos or Santorini in July.

| Cost (6 nights, 7 days) | Tight budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Beds | $150–250 | $290–365 |
| Food | $170–270 | $310–425 |
| Ferries and local transport | $130–200 | $185–285 |
| Sights | $50–100 | $155–210 |
| **Total** | **about $500–820** | **about $940–1,285** |

The low end assumes Athens dorms at €15–25 and island dorms at €25–50 a night, slow ferries at about €43 ($47) a ride and the Acropolis plus one or two other sights. Mid-range assumes a shared double, faster ferries at €60–90 a ride, two days of car or ATV hire and a boat excursion. In July and August expect the high end or more on the islands. As a check, one backpacker guide puts a day at €60–95 ($66–105) and a middle-of-the-road day at €115–165 ($127–182), both close to these figures.

### The week, day by day

#### Days 1–3: Athens

From Athens airport the metro is about €9 ($10) one way. A 90-minute ticket on all city transport is €1.20 ($1.30), a 24-hour pass is €4.10 ($4.50) and a three-day tourist ticket is €20 ($22) and includes one round trip to the airport, which is the best value if you arrive and leave by metro. The Acropolis costs €30 ($33), up from €20 since 1 April 2025. Most other historic sites are about €15 ($17) and most museums about €10 ($11), so pick a few rather than a pass. Walking the old streets, the free viewpoints and the street food are the cheap parts of Athens.

#### Days 4–6: one island

Take a ferry from Piraeus. The cheapest Piraeus to Mykonos sailings listed start at about €43 ($47), with the highest fares near €90 ($99), and slow boats take about 4.5 hours against about 2.5 hours for a high-speed one. Another guide puts a longer Athens to Milos crossing at about €70 ($77), and general ferry fares at about €25–70 ($28–77). Overnight boats can save up to half. On Mykonos, local buses cost about €1.60–2.50 ($1.75–2.75) a ride and an ATV about €30–60 ($33–66) a day, and across Greece a car is about €20–30 ($22–33) a day booked ahead.

#### Day 7: home

Fly home from the island, or take the ferry back and fly from Athens. Ferry timetables and prices change by month, so check Ferryhopper for your date before you book flights.

## Cheapest time to go

April, May, September and October are the best balance of weather and price. On Mykonos, prices from June to August can triple against spring and autumn, and in winter many businesses close. Some state sites charge about half price from November to March: for example the Acropolis of Lindos on Rhodes is about €6 ($6.60) in winter against €12 ($13) in summer. Ferries run less often outside summer, so check the schedule before you plan an island week.

## Planning Greece from your country

For most visitors the flight is the biggest single cost, so check fares from your own airport and compare more than one airline. Entry rules depend on your passport, so check the official Greek and EU border pages before you book. Ferry fares, ticket prices and the climate fee change, so confirm them on the operator and ticket sites before you go. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for 7 days in Greece?** About $500–820 for a tight budget with dorm beds and $940–1,285 at mid-range, excluding flights, based on three nights in Athens and three on one island.

**Is Greece cheap?** Athens and the mainland are among Southern Europe's cheaper places, especially street food, lunch specials and dorms. Mykonos and Santorini in July and August are where costs climb.

**How much is the Acropolis ticket?** €30 ($33) for a full-price adult ticket since 1 April 2025. Check the official site for the current rate and free-entry days.

**How much is the ferry from Athens to the islands?** From about €43 ($47) on the cheapest Piraeus to Mykonos sailings, and up to about €70–90 ($77–99) on faster or longer routes. Overnight boats can save up to half.

**When is Greece cheapest?** April, May, September and October for weather and price, and winter for the lowest prices, though many island businesses close.
